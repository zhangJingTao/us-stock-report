0. 今日一句话总结

标普500几乎平收（+0.05%），道指上涨+0.46%，但纳指与纳指100分别下跌-0.64%和-1.15%。SOXX重挫-4.40%，半导体成为主要拖累，市场从科技成长转向道指价值股。VIX报18.58，10年期美债仍处4.679%高位。下周FOMC、核心PCE及微软、Meta、苹果、亚马逊财报密集落地，事件风险高。
  • Dow rises but Nasdaq falls as chip stocks sink; major indexes log a second straight weekly loss：Friday ended mixed: the Dow gained 235.60 points (0.46%) to 51,947.25, the S&P 500 edged up 3.68 points (0.05%) to 7,411.98, while the Nasdaq declined as semiconductor weakness dominated. All three major averages finished lower for a second consecutive week.
  • Chip weakness offsets solid earnings and economic data：Semiconductors remained the principal drag on the S&P 500 and Nasdaq at the close, showing that the AI trade's leadership had become fragile even as reported corporate results and activity data were generally constructive.
  • Intel reverses early gains as foundry and AI spending concerns outweigh strong results：Intel shares fell sharply after an initially positive reaction to Q2 results. Investors focused on rising capital expenditure and foundry economics despite revenue above $16 billion and strong AI data-center growth, reinforcing scrutiny of AI spending returns.
  • Alphabet and Tesla earnings fallout leaves megacap AI spending under pressure：The market was still digesting the prior day's steep post-earnings losses in Alphabet and Tesla. Alphabet's higher spending plans and negative free cash flow renewed concern that hyperscalers are consuming cash faster than they can demonstrate AI returns; the Magnificent Seven ETF fell more than 5% for the week.
  • Oil above $100 and the U.S.-Iran conflict revive inflation and yield fears：Renewed Middle East hostilities kept crude above $100 a barrel and pushed inflation expectations and long-dated yields higher. The near-40% oil rise was a major macro overhang on equities and complicated the Fed outlook.
  • July U.S. business activity accelerates, helped by World Cup and holiday spending：S&P Global's flash survey showed faster services activity in July, partly tied to FIFA World Cup and Independence Day spending. The resilient demand signal supported growth expectations but, alongside oil-driven inflation pressure, also reinforced the risk of a hawkish Fed.
  • Fed decision is the next major test, with markets assigning a 35% chance of a July hike：The FOMC decision arrives Wednesday, July 29. Consensus expected the first hike in September, but fed-funds futures placed about 35% odds on an immediate quarter-point increase, making the statement and press conference central catalysts for rates and equities.
  • Megacap earnings deluge and key inflation data set up a high-risk week：Meta and Microsoft report Wednesday; Amazon and Apple Thursday, alongside Qualcomm and Lam Research. Thursday also brings Q2 GDP, June core PCE, income and spending, followed Friday by ECI and Michigan sentiment—an unusually dense cluster of AI, growth and inflation catalysts.
